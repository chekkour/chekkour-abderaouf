Mapping territorial vulnerability to wildfires

Using remote sensing, geographic information systems (GIS) and the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), we studied territorial vulnerability to wildfires in Tizi Ouzou, Algeria, with a focus on the August 2021 fires. The research examines steep terrain, the wildland-urban interface and the role of territorial planning in protecting people and resources.

Research abstract

Forests in Algeria, particularly in Tizi Ouzou Province, face a growing threat from catastrophic wildfires. The challenge extends beyond ignition to rapid fire spread and the difficulty of bringing fires under control, especially where settlements and farms are interwoven with forest land. At this wildland-urban interface, fire threatens human lives and property as well as ecosystems and vegetation cover.

In response to the limitations of conventional approaches, our research aims to assess territorial vulnerability and develop a smart spatial decision-support tool. We adopted an integrated methodology combining remote sensing, geographic information systems (GIS) and the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) to model fire risk.

Our analysis of the exceptional August 2021 fires identifies steep terrain and wildland-urban interface areas as major contributors to the persistence of catastrophic fires. The study therefore calls for a shift toward proactive, informed territorial planning to protect people and resources. It recommends using geospatial techniques to address the structural vulnerability of mountainous regions and help prevent similar disasters.

Keywords: Forest fires; Territorial vulnerability; Wildland-urban interface (WUI); Geospatial techniques; Geographic information systems (GIS); Remote sensing; Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP); Tizi Ouzou Province.
